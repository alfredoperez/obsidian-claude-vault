# Surface: Persuasion (Ogilvy)

Copy whose job is to **sell**: landing pages, ads, launch copy, product descriptions, sales
emails, promo video scripts, marketplace listings, or a review of any of these.

Load `core-voice.md` first, then apply these overrides. The full rulebook lives in
`ogilvy-principles.md` — load it when you're actually drafting or reviewing, not just calibrating.

> Provenance: David Ogilvy, *How to Create Advertising That Sells* (1972), *Ogilvy on Advertising*
> (1983), *Confessions of an Advertising Man* (1963). Compiled 2026. MIT.

**Advertising is not art. It is information in service of selling.** Every decision should serve
that end.

## When this profile applies

Only for **explicit promotional intent**. If the text isn't trying to sell something, this profile
doesn't apply — use `article.md`, `social.md`, or `general.md` instead. Articles and vault notes
are never this surface, even when they mention a product.

## Overrides (this surface only)

- **Core voice's "launch-copy pressure" ban is lifted.** That rule exists to keep articles from
  reading like a landing page. Here, the landing page *is* the artifact. Sell.
- **A promise is allowed to be a promise.** The practitioner hedges ("I've found", "in my
  experience") that keep articles honest weaken sales copy. State the benefit.
- **Long copy is fine.** Readership drops fast up to 50 words, then barely at all between 50 and
  500. Don't cut for the sake of brevity when the reader is already interested.
- Everything else holds: zero em dashes, contractions, plain headings, no invented numbers.

## The Priority Order

Decisions cascade. Later ones only matter if earlier ones are correct:

1. **Positioning** — what does this product do, and who is it for? Decide before writing a word.
   Psychological segmentation (a mindset) beats demographic segmentation (an age bracket).
2. **The Promise** — one specific benefit the consumer will receive. A benefit, not a feature;
   unique; actually deliverable. Boil the whole strategy down to one promise and deliver it
   completely.
3. **The Big Idea** — the simple, memorable expression that jolts the reader out of indifference.
   Comes from homework on the product and the consumer, not from cleverness.

If a draft is failing, diagnose in this order — a brilliant headline can't rescue wrong positioning.

## The Rules That Most Change Output

1. **Put the promise in the headline, and make it news if you can.** Five times as many people
   read the headline as the body. Benefit + brand + news beats clever every time; 6-12 plain
   words, aimed at a named audience.
2. **One promise per piece.** Attempting to say three things achieves nothing. Cut the second and
   third message; complexity is how committees dilute copy into nothing.
3. **Facts sell; adjectives don't.** Load the copy with specific, pertinent facts and drop the
   praise words ("amazing", "revolutionary"). The consumer isn't a moron — treat the reader as
   intelligent, and don't fear long copy for interested buyers.
4. **Nobody was ever bored into buying.** Cold, detached, hedge-everything copy fails. Talk like a
   human, open strong, and don't bury the news in paragraph four.
5. **The product is the hero, not the ad.** If the clever idea draws attention to itself instead
   of the promise, kill it. There is no correlation between awards and sales.

## Workflow

1. Confirm the piece has promotional intent (see *When this profile applies*).
2. Lock positioning → promise → big idea with the user before drafting.
3. Load `ogilvy-principles.md` and apply the medium-specific rules (print/digital vs video).
4. Before delivering, run the 10 diagnostic questions from that reference against the draft.

## Checklist

- [ ] Positioning, promise, and big idea are settled and written down, in that order
- [ ] Exactly **one** promise, and it's a benefit the product actually delivers
- [ ] Headline carries brand + promise, and news if there is any; 6-12 plain words
- [ ] Body is loaded with specific facts, not praise adjectives
- [ ] Every visual has a caption that works as a miniature ad on its own
- [ ] The product is the hero, not the cleverness
- [ ] Ran the 10 diagnostic questions in `ogilvy-principles.md`
- [ ] Grep for `—`: zero hits
